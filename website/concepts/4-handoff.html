Your phone puts it first: Waiting on you.

Why Lostty exists

Coding agents work best when you let them run. The trouble is, they don't run for long before they need you. I built Lostty to fix the things that kept going wrong whenever I left an agent running and walked away.

#1

The agent stopped, and I didn't know.

The problemYou start Claude on something big. You go make a coffee. Twenty minutes later you come back, and it's been sitting on a permission prompt for nineteen of them.

The fixLostty knows what every terminal is doing. Your phone sorts them into Waiting on you, Working, Ready and Idle, so the one that needs you is always first. Tap in, answer, and Claude carries on. Anything that waited on you while you were busy lands in the inbox under the bell.

#2

My phone got a tiny, useless Mac.

The problemScreen sharing works, technically. But you're pinching around a 27-inch screen on a 6-inch phone, and typing into it is miserable.

The fixThe phone app is a real terminal. It opens the same session that's running on your Mac: same output, same prompt, same half-typed command. Come back to your Mac and carry on. Nothing restarts. The keyboard is made for it, too: a tap that lands in the gap between two keys types the nearest one, instead of nothing.

Turn on Stay Awake before you leave
  • Your Mac keeps working with the lid closed, even on battery.
  • If its Wi-Fi drops, it hops onto your iPhone's hotspot, so you can still reach it.

Ten terminals, all the same black box.

The problemOnce you've got a few agents going, every terminal looks the same. Sooner or later, you type into the wrong one.

The fixSkins. Each project gets its own colors and texture, and the terminal changes to match when you move into a project. Your phone shows the same colors, so the list reads at a glance. Each terminal also says what Claude is doing right now, like Editing App.swift, with its live ✳ Working… line. A soft glow traces the window while Claude works, so you can tell from across the room.

Lostty started from Ghostty, the fast, native terminal by Mitchell Hashimoto and the Ghostty contributors. Every character you see is drawn by Ghostty's engine, which does the hard part quickly and correctly. Lostty adds the Mac and iPhone side on top.

Getting Started

Lostty is a Mac terminal with an iPhone app. You build it on your Mac, install the phone app over the cable, and pair the two inside your own Tailscale network. Plan on an hour, most of it waiting for the first build.

You'll need: a Mac on macOS 15 or later, an iPhone on iOS 17 or later with its cable, and a free Apple Account.

On your Mac

1Install Xcode 26

Get it from the App Store and open it once. You'll also need Homebrew.

2Install Lostty

In Terminal:

Terminal
git clone https://github.com/Lawliang/lostty.git
cd lostty
macos/setup.sh

It installs what the build needs (it may ask for your password), then builds Lostty, puts it in /Applications and opens it. The first build takes a while.

Updating laterRun git pull, then macos/install.sh, then choose Restart to Update in Lostty's menu bar. Don't Quit Lostty to update: quitting ends every terminal session.

On your iPhone

3Add your Apple Account to Xcode

In Xcode: Settings → Accounts, click +, and sign in.

4Install the app

Plug your iPhone into the Mac, unlock it, and tap Trust. Then, in the lostty folder:

Terminal
macos/install-phone.sh

The first time, the phone may ask you to:

  • turn on Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode (the phone restarts);
  • trust your Apple Account in Settings → General → VPN & Device Management.

Then run the command again.

With a free Apple Account, the app stops opening after 7 daysRun the command again to renew it. A paid developer account makes it last a year.

Pair them

5Install Tailscale on both

Install Tailscale on the Mac and the iPhone, and sign in to both with the same account. Your phone talks to your Mac directly, over SSH, inside your own private Tailscale network. There's no Lostty server in the middle, and the phone's SSH key never leaves the phone.

6Pair

On the Mac, open Lostty → Phone Access… and fix any row without a ✓. Each row says what to click. Then click Pair a Phone…, and on the iPhone open Lostty, tap Pair with Mac and scan the code. Click Allow on the Mac.

That's it. Go take a walk.

Your phone reaches the Mac while it's awake and Lostty is open. To keep it reachable with the lid closed, turn on Stay Awake in Lostty's menu bar, and see Lostty on your iPhone.
