Why Lostty exists

Coding agents work best when you let them run. They don't run for long before they need you. Lostty fixes the four things that kept going wrong whenever I left an agent running and walked away.

#1

The agent stopped, and I didn't know

ProblemYou start Claude on something big. You go make a coffee. Twenty minutes later you come back, and it's been sitting on a permission prompt for nineteen of them.

FixLostty knows what every terminal is doing. Your phone sorts them into Waiting on you, Working, Ready and Idle, so the one that needs you is always first. Tap in, answer, and Claude carries on. Anything that waited on you while you were busy lands in the inbox under the bell.

1 Waiting on youlostty
2 Workingapi-server
3 Readydocs-site
4 Idledotfiles

The axolotl tells you at a glance. Its eyes are a prompt: > and a blinking — cursor.

#2

My phone got a tiny, useless Mac

ProblemScreen sharing works, technically. But you're pinching around a 27-inch screen on a 6-inch phone, and typing into it is miserable.

FixLostty's phone app is a real terminal. It opens the same session that's running on your Mac: same output, same prompt, same half-typed command. Come back to your Mac and carry on. Nothing restarts.

The keyboard is made for it, too. A tap that lands in the gap between two keys types the nearest one, instead of nothing.

Turn on Stay Awake in Lostty's menu bar before you leave.
  • Your Mac keeps working with the lid closed, even on battery.
  • If its Wi-Fi drops, it hops onto your iPhone's hotspot, so you can still reach it.

#3

Ten terminals, all the same black box

ProblemOnce you've got a few agents going, every terminal looks the same. Sooner or later, you type into the wrong one.

FixSkins. Each project gets its own colors and texture, and the terminal changes to match when you move into a project. Pick from twelve built-in skins, or make your own. Your phone shows the same colors, so the list reads at a glance.

Each terminal also says what Claude is doing right now, in plain words, like Editing App.swift, with its live ✳ Working… line. A soft glow traces the window while Claude works, so you can tell from across the room.

Getting Started

Lostty runs on your Mac. The iPhone app connects to it over your own Tailscale network. Setup is six steps, and most of them are one command.

What you'll need

  • A Mac on macOS 15 or later.
  • An iPhone on iOS 17 or later, with its cable.
  • A free Apple Account.

On your Mac

  1. Install Xcode 26

    Get it from the App Store and open it once. You'll also need Homebrew.

  2. Install Lostty

    In Terminal:

    sh
    git clone https://github.com/Lawliang/lostty.git
cd lostty
macos/setup.sh

    It installs what the build needs (it may ask for your password), then builds Lostty, puts it in /Applications and opens it. The first build takes a while.

    Updating later

    Run git pull, then macos/install.sh, then choose Restart to Update in Lostty's menu bar. Don't Quit Lostty to update: quitting ends every terminal session.

On your iPhone

  1. Add your Apple Account to Xcode

    In Xcode: Settings → Accounts, click +, and sign in.

  2. Install the app

    Plug your iPhone into the Mac, unlock it, and tap Trust. Then, in the lostty folder:

    sh
    macos/install-phone.sh

    The first time, the phone may ask you to:

    • turn on Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode (the phone restarts);
    • trust your Apple Account in Settings → General → VPN & Device Management.

    Then run the command again.

    Free accounts

    With a free Apple Account, the app stops opening after 7 days. Run the command again to renew it. A paid developer account makes it last a year.

Pair them

  1. Install Tailscale on both

    Install Tailscale on the Mac and the iPhone, and sign in to both with the same account.

    Why Tailscale?

    Your phone talks to your Mac directly, over SSH, inside your own private Tailscale network. There's no Lostty server in the middle. There isn't one. And the phone's SSH key never leaves the phone.

  2. Pair

    On the Mac, open Lostty → Phone Access… and fix any row without a ✓. Each row says what to click. Then click Pair a Phone…, and on the iPhone open Lostty, tap Pair with Mac and scan the code. Click Allow on the Mac.

    If you'd rather set up the Mac by hand, the steps Phone Access automates are in Manual Mac setup.

That's it. Go take a walk.

Your phone reaches the Mac while it's awake and Lostty is open. To keep it reachable with the lid closed, turn on Stay Awake.

Stay Awake

Install the Stay-awake permission in Phone Access, then choose Stay Awake for Phone from Lostty's menu bar icon. It turns itself off at your battery floor (15% by default) or if the Mac gets too hot.

Phone Access

Open your Mac's Lostty sessions from your iPhone, anywhere, over your own Tailscale. Nothing goes through a Lostty server.

Set up once

On the Mac, open Lostty → Phone Access… and fix any row that isn't ✓:

  • tmux: brew install tmux.
  • Remote Login and Password logins off: one admin password prompt turns on Remote Login and allows only key logins.
  • Terminal definition: click Install.
  • Folder access (optional): click Grant Now, so commands from your phone never wait on a macOS prompt.

Then click Pair a Phone…. On the iPhone, tap Pair with Mac and scan the code.

Using it

The phone lists your Mac's sessions and keeps the list current while it's open. A Claude Code session shows its task, with Waiting when it's waiting for you or Working while it works. Waiting sessions come first. Opening one on the phone counts as seeing it, on the Mac too.

Swipe left on a row and tap Rename to name a session. It's the same name as Rename Tab… on the Mac.

Lost phone

Open Lostty → Phone Access on the Mac and click Remove next to it. If you can't get to Lostty, delete the line ending in lostty-phone:<id> from ~/.ssh/authorized_keys.

Turning it off

Phone Access → Turn Off Phone Access… removes every paired phone and offers to turn password logins back on.

Skins

Lostty gives every pane a background color and texture based on the project it's in. cd into a project and the pane re-skins; leave and it reverts.

The twelve presets

SkinRarityTexturePatron

Override a pane

Click the Skins icon in the sidebar, or use the skins command in any pane:

sh
skins set silverbow
skins color '#3a0f14'
skins texture grid
skins reset

Map folders to skins

Copy docs/skins/skins.example.toml to ~/.config/lostty/skins/skins.toml. A [[match]] entry gives a folder its default skin. Errors never break the terminal: the last good config stays active.

Claude Trace

While Claude Code works in a pane, a light ray races around its edge. Each preset has its own trace. With Reduce Motion on, the border glows gently instead.

Manual Mac setup

Most people should use Lostty → Phone Access. These are the steps it automates, for setting up by hand.

Steps

  1. tmux

    brew install tmux

  2. Remote Login

    System Settings → General → Sharing → Remote Login on, with "Allow access for" set to only your user.

  3. Keys only

    Create /etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/000-lostty.conf containing:

    sshd_config
    PasswordAuthentication no
KbdInteractiveAuthentication no

    Toggle Remote Login off and on, then check that ssh -o PreferredAuthentications=password localhost refuses.

  4. The phone's key

    In the phone app, Settings → Show This Phone's Key. Append it to ~/.ssh/authorized_keys as restrict,pty <key> lostty-phone:<any-id>, then chmod 600 ~/.ssh/authorized_keys.

  5. Tailscale

    On the Mac and the iPhone, signed in to the same tailnet, starting at login, with two-factor on the account.

Awake in a bag

sudo pmset -a disablesleep 1 keeps the Mac awake with the lid closed. Expect heat under heavy builds, and run it with 0 to undo.

Contributing

Bug reports, ideas and changes are all welcome.

Report a bug

Open an issue at github.com/Lawliang/lostty/issues. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened, plus your macOS version (and iOS version for the phone app). If it also happens in plain Ghostty, it belongs upstream.

Build

You'll need Xcode 26, the Metal Toolchain, Zig 0.15.2 and Nushell. Build the engine first, then the Mac app:

sh
brew install zig@0.15 nushell tmux
zig build -Demit-macos-app=false
macos/build.nu

Send a change

  1. Fork the repo and make a branch.
  2. Keep the change focused: one fix or feature per pull request.
  3. Run the tests that cover what you touched.
  4. Open a pull request saying what changed and why.
