Why Lostty exists

Coding agents work best when you let them run. They don't run for long before they need you: they stop to ask for permission, or a question, and then they wait. Lostty fixes the four things that kept going wrong whenever I walked away.

#1

The agent stopped, and I didn't know

The problemYou start Claude on something big and go make a coffee. Twenty minutes later it has been sitting on a permission prompt for nineteen of them.

The fixLostty knows what every terminal is doing. Your phone sorts them into Waiting on you, Working, Ready and Idle, so the one that needs you is always first. Anything that waited while you were busy lands in the inbox under the bell.

#2

My phone got a tiny, useless Mac

The problemScreen sharing works, technically. But you're pinching around a 27-inch screen on a 6-inch phone, and typing into it is miserable.

The fixThe phone app is a real terminal on the same session that's running on your Mac: same output, same prompt, same half-typed command. Come back to your desk and carry on. Nothing restarts.

Turn on Stay Awake before you leave. Your Mac keeps working with the lid closed, even on battery, and if its Wi-Fi drops it hops onto your iPhone's hotspot.

#3

Ten terminals, all the same black box

The problemOnce you've got a few agents going, every terminal looks the same. Sooner or later, you type into the wrong one.

The fixEach project gets its own colors and texture, and the terminal changes to match when you move into it. Each one says what Claude is doing in plain words, like Editing App.swift, and a soft glow traces the window while Claude works, so you can tell from across the room.

#4

Starting anything meant going back to my desk

The problemYou're out, and you think of the next thing. Now you either wait until you're back, or you go back.

The fixTap + on your phone and pick a project folder on your Mac. Choose what starts there: Claude, Codex or a plain shell, and which model. It opens on your Mac and on your phone at the same time.

Built on Ghostty

Lostty started from Ghostty, the fast, native terminal by Mitchell Hashimoto and the Ghostty contributors. Every character you see is drawn by Ghostty's engine, which does the hard part quickly and correctly. Lostty adds the Mac and iPhone side on top.

If you love how fast Lostty feels, that's Ghostty. Go give it a star. Lostty is MIT licensed, like Ghostty before it.

Docs / Getting Started

Getting Started

Install Lostty on your Mac, put the app on your iPhone, and pair them. You build both from source, so you'll need Xcode.

What you'll need

  • A Mac on macOS 15 or later.
  • An iPhone on iOS 17 or later, with its cable.
  • A free Apple Account.

On your Mac

1Install Xcode 26

Get it from the App Store and open it once. You'll also need Homebrew.

2Install Lostty

In Terminal:

sh
git clone https://github.com/Lawliang/lostty.git
cd lostty
macos/setup.sh

It installs what the build needs (it may ask for your password), then builds Lostty, puts it in /Applications and opens it. The first build takes a while.

On your iPhone

3Add your Apple Account to Xcode

In Xcode: Settings → Accounts, click +, and sign in.

4Install the app

Plug your iPhone into the Mac, unlock it, and tap Trust. Then, in the lostty folder:

sh
macos/install-phone.sh

The first time, the phone may ask you to:

  • turn on Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode (the phone restarts);
  • trust your Apple Account in Settings → General → VPN & Device Management.

Then run the command again.

With a free Apple Account

The app stops opening after 7 days. Run macos/install-phone.sh again to renew it. A paid developer account makes it last a year.

Pair them

5Install Tailscale on both

Install Tailscale on the Mac and the iPhone, and sign in to both with the same account.

Why Tailscale?

Your phone talks to your Mac directly, over SSH, inside your own private Tailscale network. There's no Lostty server in the middle. There isn't one. And the phone's SSH key never leaves the phone.

6Pair

On the Mac, open Lostty → Phone Access… and fix any row without a ✓. Each row says what to click. Then click Pair a Phone…, and on the iPhone open Lostty, tap Pair with Mac and scan the code. Click Allow on the Mac.

That's it. Go take a walk. Your phone reaches the Mac while it's awake and Lostty is open. To keep it reachable with the lid closed, see Phone Access.

Updating later

sh
git pull
macos/install.sh

Then choose Restart to Update in Lostty's menu bar.

Don't Quit to update

Quitting Lostty ends every terminal session. Restart to Update keeps them.

Docs / Phone Access

Lostty on your iPhone

Open your Mac's Lostty sessions from your iPhone, anywhere, over your own Tailscale. Nothing goes through a Lostty server; there isn't one.

Set up (once)

On the Mac, open Lostty → Phone Access… and fix any row that isn't ✓:

  • tmux: brew install tmux.
  • Remote Login and Password logins off: one admin password prompt turns on Remote Login and allows only key logins.
  • Terminal definition: click Install.
  • Folder access (optional): click Grant Now, so commands from your phone never wait on a macOS prompt.

Lid closed

Install the Stay-awake permission in Phone Access, then choose Stay Awake for Phone from Lostty's menu bar icon. It turns itself off at your battery floor (15% by default) or if the Mac gets too hot.

Using it

A Claude Code session shows its task, with Waiting when it's waiting for you or Working while it works. Waiting sessions come first. Opening one on the phone counts as seeing it, on the Mac too.

Lost phone

Open Lostty → Phone Access on the Mac and click Remove next to it.

Docs / Skins

Skins

Lostty gives every pane a background color and texture based on the project it is in.

How it works

  • cd into a project and the pane re-skins; leave and it reverts.
  • Split panes are skinned independently.
  • Git repos you haven't configured get a stable automatic skin.

Override a pane

sh
skins set neon-arcade
skins color '#3a0f14'
skins texture grid
skins reset

Claude Trace

While Claude Code is working in a pane, a light ray races around that pane's edge. The moment Claude finishes, or asks for permission, the ray disappears. Each preset has its own trace.

Docs / Manual Mac setup

Manual Mac setup

Phone Access sets up your Mac for you. If you'd rather do it by hand, these are the steps it automates.

What Phone Access changes

  • Installs tmux and the terminal definition.
  • Turns on Remote Login and, if you agree, turns password logins off with /etc/ssh/sshd_config.d/000-lostty.conf.
  • Adds each paired phone's key to ~/.ssh/authorized_keys, on a line ending in lostty-phone:<id>.

The full walkthrough is in docs/lostty-mobile-host-setup.md.

Docs / Contributing

Contributing

Bug reports, ideas and changes are all welcome. Lostty has two halves: the terminal engine (Zig, in src/) and the apps (Swift, in macos/).

Prerequisites

  • Xcode 26 and the Metal Toolchain: xcodebuild -downloadComponent MetalToolchain
  • Zig 0.15.2 and Nushell: brew install zig@0.15 nushell
  • tmux, for the phone features

Build

sh
zig build -Demit-macos-app=false
macos/build.nu

If a bug also happens in plain Ghostty (rendering, fonts, escape sequences, input), it belongs upstream.
